# Roadmap

The goal: everything you'd expect from a premium life sim plus its paid expansions,
in one free generator, with original content throughout. This page tracks what's built and
what's next. Inspired-by is about *systems*; names, text and events are written fresh.

## Built (v0.1)

**Life & stats.** Random or custom births in 18 countries, birthdays and zodiac signs,
happiness / health / smarts / looks plus karma and fame (visible, not hidden), 20
personality traits that change odds, 13 skills, a yearly life log with stat-change chips.

**Family.** Parents (including two moms or two dads), older siblings, grandparents,
in-laws, step-parents, grandchildren. Inheritance follows your will. When you die you can
continue as any of your children, across unlimited generations, with a family-line view.

**Relationships.** 30+ interactions (time together, gifts, arguments, pranks, hugs,
asking for money...). Friends, best friends, enemies, classmates, coworkers, bosses,
cellmates. Dating (find love, dating app, hookups) with orientation-aware matching and a
hard teen/adult line. Proposals with ring tiers, four wedding sizes, affairs, cheating,
breakups, divorce with asset splits. Babies (fertility by age, twins, miscarriage),
adoption, sexuality and gender identity (he / she / they).

**Pets.** 15 species, horses included: play, walk or ride, vet visits, rehoming, natural
lifespans.

**School.** Primary, middle and high school with grades, cliques and 12 clubs. Study, skip,
talk to teachers, report cards, prom, dropping out, GED. University (30 majors), community
college, trade school (10 trades), and grad school (law, medicine, dental, pharmacy, vet,
MBA, master's, PhD). Paid for by parents, scholarships, loans or cash. Probation, expulsion,
honors.

**Careers.** 70+ career ladders across 16 fields, plus interviews, performance, raises,
promotions, firing, retirement and pensions. Also 14 part-time jobs and 15 side gigs. Special tracks:
- **Fame:** acting, music, modeling, writing, comedy (big breaks, tours, royalties).
- **Sports:** pro athlete and fighter (seasons, injuries, fights).
- **Military:** enlisted and officer ranks, deployments, medals.
- **Politics:** elections, campaign budgets, re-election, town halls, scandals, bribes.

**Money.** Country tax rates, lifestyle tiers, rent and upkeep. Loans: personal, student,
mortgage, car, boat. Five investment types, voluntary and forced bankruptcy, trust funds for
minors, sponsorship income.

**Assets.** 15 home types, from studios to castles plus duplexes, fourplexes and apartment
buildings (buy with cash or a mortgage, move in, rent out, renovate, throw parties). Rentals, 13 car classes, boats, aircraft, jewelry, instruments, art. Condition and
market value change over time; cars can crash.

**Health.** 40+ conditions, including hidden cancers that checkups catch early, plus
injuries and STIs. Four treatment tiers, near-free care in countries with universal
healthcare, therapy, plastic surgery, rehab, six addictions, diets, salons.

**Crime.** 11 solo crimes plus assault and murder. Cold cases, trials with four lawyer
tiers or a plea deal, sentencing, juvenile detention. Prison life: workouts, library, jobs,
GED, gangs, riots, escapes, appeals, parole hearings. Fugitive status.

**Activities.** Gym, running, meditation, library, reading, yoga, gardening, 12 classes.
Social life: meeting people, nightlife, parties, concerts, volunteering, movies, theme
parks, shopping. The lottery, a casino (blackjack, roulette, slots), charity, 12 vacations,
emigration, driver's license, name changes. Social media: posts, going viral,
verification, sponsorships. Fan meets and publicity stunts.

**Events.** Over 500 random events across every stage of life and in prison (see More story).

**Business ownership (v0.2).** Start up to three companies across 22 industries, from food
trucks and bakeries to tech startups, biotech, hotels and airlines. Medical, dental, law and
biotech firms need the matching degree. Fund the start with savings, a personally guaranteed
loan, an angel investor or family.
- **Running it:** hire five kinds of staff in bulk (with recruiting costs and pay levels),
  then handle raises, training, perks, layoffs, morale and turnover.
- **Growing it:** open locations (with market saturation), launch products whose quality
  depends on your R&D team, budget and skills, and set pricing tiers plus marketing and R&D budgets.
- **Autopilot hiring** only hires when the forecast says each hire pays for itself. The
  company sheet shows a projected P&L, how much one more hire would add, and plain-language
  warnings.
- **Investors:** funding rounds dilute your stake. Once investors own the majority, two
  losing years can get you ousted, and you can fight to win your seat back.
- **Money out:** business loans, your own salary, dividends.
- **Going public:** IPOs with a ticker and a yearly share price. Sell shares, take buyout
  offers, shut down, or go bankrupt (and owe what you guaranteed).
- **Family business:** companies pass whole to the heir you name (see Parenting), or else to the
  child you continue as, with a trustee until they turn 18.
- **Extras:** 22 business events (inspections, data breaches, unions, lawsuits, poaching,
  activist investors, buyouts...), 10 achievements, 3 goals, and a Business Mogul legacy title.

**Business depth.** A living market of 66 named rival companies (three per industry, from
local shops to industry giants) that grow, shrink, go public and go bankrupt every year, and
outlast you across generations.
- **Competition:** each company page ranks you against your rivals by revenue and market
  share. Rivals show up by name in events: hit launches, scandals, price wars, poaching,
  stores opening next door. Big rivals make the buyout offers.
- **Stock market:** buy and sell shares of listed rivals, browse by industry, biggest movers
  or biggest companies. Prices follow a shared market (with booms and crashes), each
  company's growth, and what it's really worth. Dividends, 15% tax on gains, and shares
  that go to zero when a company fails. The index fund now tracks the same market.
- **Acquisitions:** buy any company outright and run it yourself or keep its CEO, or merge a
  rival into your company to absorb its locations and staff (paid from company cash, your
  own money, or a loan). Owning most of a company lets you take over as CEO at any time.
- **Franchising:** restaurant-style and service businesses (food trucks, cafés, bakeries,
  restaurants, salons, gyms, real estate, hotels) can franchise once they have 3 locations,
  reputation 55+ and a profitable year. Pick a 4%, 6% or 9% royalty. Outlets grow with your
  reputation, and franchisee scandals can hurt the brand.
- **Extras:** 5 new company events and 4 new achievements (Hostile Takeover, Franchise King,
  Market Leader, Stock Picker).

**Meta.** 122 achievements kept across lives, three bucket-list goals per life, legacy titles
and epitaphs, the Hall of Lives, multiple save slots, export/import codes. God Mode (stats,
money, skills, traits, instant careers and degrees) and a 10-year Time Machine, both free.
Light/dark themes, text size, reduced motion.
- **God Mode for anyone:** every living person's page has an Edit button: name, gender,
  orientation, age, stats, relationship (friend, best friend or enemy), personality, money, job
  and rung, education, home country and city, and their whole appearance, body included for
  adults. Ages keep families sane (parents stay older than their kids) and romance stays on your
  side of 18.
- **God Mode buttons** can be switched off in Settings, which hides God Mode from the menu,
  people's pages, your companies and the custom-life stat picker.

**Content tags.** Events, choices and actions can be tagged `chaos` (absurd and
supernatural), `mature` (harder crime and affair content), or `taboo` + one subtype
(parent, step, sibling, child, grand, extended, in-law) for consensual adult-only family
romance. Each tag has an off-by-default switch in Settings, so players who want the game
weirder or darker can opt in. The taboo master plus seven subtype switches live in their
own Settings section and all default OFF, so the game is exactly as before until opted in.
So far, seven events are tagged `chaos` (four of them toddler hazards), and three of the
messiest affair and one-night-stand events are tagged `mature` (see Relationships 2.0).

**Quick wins.** Petty actions: throw a tantrum to squeeze money out of your parents,
toilet-paper someone's house, yell at people (detention at school, a write-up at work), spread
rumors, give the silent treatment, and play ding-dong ditch. Seven toddler hazards where the
risky choice can end your life early: three slapstick ones (a fork in the socket, climbing
the fridge, the ceiling fan), plus four `chaos` ones (a glowing marble, single combat with a
goose, floating off on party balloons, a beehive piñata). A prison economy:
- **Trading:** a contraband dealer (noodles, candy, hooch, shivs, phones) with resale prices
  that change every year. Gangs get better prices.
- **Using your stash:** brew hooch, call home on a smuggled phone, eat or share contraband
  snacks, and carry a shiv that wins fights.
- **Risk:** cell searches, a crooked guard, and lockdowns that drive prices up. Getting caught
  with a phone or a shiv can add a year to your sentence.
- **Romance:** pen-pal relationships and weddings in the prison chapel.

**Royalty.** Royal families in the United Kingdom, Spain, Sweden and Japan, each with a
fictional royal house and palace. About 3% of random births there are royal, or pick
"Royal family" on the custom-life screen. You're born the monarch's child or grandchild,
somewhere in the line of succession.
- **Titles and succession:** King or Queen (Emperor or Empress in Japan), Crown Prince or
  Princess, Prince or Princess, consorts, and former monarchs. Monarchs get regnal numbers
  (King Emil II), and the crown has a history of earlier reigns. The eldest line inherits
  regardless of gender. When a monarch dies or abdicates, the crown passes, with a coronation
  if it's you. With no heirs left, it goes to a distant cousin.
- **Royal life:** royal engagements, charity patronages, royal tours, addressing the nation,
  knighting people, a crown-funded royal wedding, a tell-all interview, giving up your title,
  and abdicating.
- **Standing:** public approval rises with duties and falls with scandals, idleness or prison.
  Working royals get a yearly allowance and stay famous. A deeply unpopular monarch can face
  a referendum that ends the monarchy.
- **Extras:** 10 royal events (paparazzi, banquet gaffes, falling for a commoner, sibling
  scandals, affair rumors, clashes with the prime minister, a Silver Jubilee...), 4
  achievements, and Monarch and Royal legacy titles.

**Organized crime.** A new Underworld menu.
- **Crime families:** every country has three rival families named after local surnames. Get
  in with one, and rise from Associate to Soldier, Capo, Underboss and Boss by earning
  respect. Getting "made" means proving yourself on a dangerous job first. An underboss takes
  over when the boss falls, or you can stage a coup. You can also break away and found your
  own family, or walk away (they may never forgive you).
- **Running things:** family jobs, a crew of soldiers on payroll, rackets that pay every year
  (protection, gambling dens, chop shops, union kickbacks, smuggling, a casino skim), and turf
  wars that can wipe out a rival family.
- **Heists:** a warehouse, an armored truck, a museum masterpiece, a tycoon held for ransom, a
  casino vault and an airport cargo hold. Go in loud, pay an inside man, or plan for months.
- **Heat:** everything draws police attention. It cools over time (faster if you lay low or
  run a museum) and turns into raids and arrests while it's high. Bribe a detective, deal with
  a rat in your crew, or become an informant yourself, with witness protection if the family
  finds out.
- **Underworld careers:** hitman contracts, a loan-shark book with deadbeats to lean on, and
  dealing from corner dealer up to cartel boss.
- **Black market:** stolen art and antiquities that gain value over time. Sell them through a
  fence or risk an auction. Open a private museum as a respectable front. Your collection and
  loan book count toward net worth.
- **Street racing:** bet on races in your own car, with crashes, police and challengers.
- **Extras:** 8 underworld events, 6 achievements (Made, The Don, Kingpin, The Professional,
  Curator of Crime, Street King) and The Don legacy title.

**Fame depth.** Every fame career now makes named work: albums, books, comedy specials, films
(new **film director** career), records for other artists (new **music producer** career),
film and TV roles, and modeling campaigns.
- **Releasing:** pick a genre (trends shift every year, and sticking with one builds mastery),
  a budget, and a title. Critics score it, and it charts: the album chart, the bestseller list,
  the streaming charts, or the box office. Hits earn promotions, and flops cost you fame.
- **Deals:** record deals, publishers, streaming networks and studios. A major deal has big
  reach and a small royalty, an indie sits in the middle, and staying independent pays the
  most if anyone finds you. If you own a record label or film studio from the business
  system, you can release through it and the money goes to your company. Agents (three tiers)
  take a cut and open doors.
- **After release:** a yearly award season at fictional award shows, back-catalog royalties
  every year (classics keep selling), talk-show appearances, and the press: paparazzi, feuds,
  dating rumors, leaks, presenting at award shows, superfans, and hosting your own show.
- **Extras:** 7 events, 6 achievements and the Living Legend legacy title.

**Sports depth.** Pro athletes pick one of six sports (soccer, basketball, American football,
baseball, hockey, cricket), each with a fictional 12-team league named after your country's
cities that lives on across generations.
- **Seasons:** team records, standings, a playoff champion, and stat lines that fit the sport
  ("17.1 points, 5.5 rebounds and 3.5 assists a game"), plus career totals. Rookie of the
  Year, All-Star and MVP awards, and championships.
- **Contracts:** free agency when your contract runs out (re-sign or chase a contender), trade
  requests, and getting traded in a blockbuster deal.
- **Fighters:** a weight class, a top-15 ranking, named opponents, training camps, title shots,
  world title belts and defenses.
- **Racing driver:** a new career from the Karting Championship through Junior Formula and
  Formula Two to the Grand Prix World Series, simulated race by race against a 20-car field.
  Wins, podiums, sponsor bonuses, crashes, faster teams signing you, and world titles.
- **After your playing days:** new coaching (assistant to championship coach) and front-office
  (scout to president of operations) careers, where your team's record gets you promoted or
  fired. Retired pros get coaching offers and can be voted into the Hall of Fame.
- **Owning a team:** a new Sports Franchise business type you can start (as an expansion team)
  or buy from the market, with championship seasons of its own.
- **Extras:** 6 events (endorsements, doping offers, press-conference trash talk, young fans...),
  7 achievements and The GOAT legacy title.

**Courts.** A new Courts menu. Sue people (from their page), companies, your employer, the city,
or even the prison you're in, and get sued yourself.
- **Lawyers:** represent yourself (a law degree and charisma help), hire a no-win, no-fee lawyer
  who takes 35% of what you win, or pay a budget, experienced or elite team a retainer plus a
  yearly bill. Run out of money and your lawyer drops you.
- **A case's life:** a trial a year or two out, discovery that can help or hurt, settlement offers
  you can take, counter or turn down, and a day in court where you choose how to testify. Juries
  sometimes add punitive damages against big companies. Whoever loses can appeal. People can
  only pay what they have, and some countersue.
- **Getting sued:** car crashes, neighbors, dog bites, malpractice claims against doctors, nurses
  and lawyers, defamation suits once you're famous, copyright claims against your latest album or
  book, frivolous strangers, and customers suing your company (the company pays the lawyers and
  any judgment).
- **Small claims court:** no lawyers, small stakes, and a verdict the same afternoon.
- **Judges:** a docket every year that you rule on: criminal trials and lawsuits, then appeals,
  then the Supreme Court's landmark questions. Rulings that get overturned, and cases left
  unheard, hurt your reputation. Good judges get appointed up the ladder, then nominated to the
  Supreme Court and grilled at a televised confirmation hearing. Plus conflicts of interest and
  envelopes of cash.
- **Lawyers:** take on clients (criminal defense, no-win no-fee suits, corporate work, pro bono),
  go to trial or make a deal, build a trial record, and defend celebrities in the trial of the year.
- **Extras:** represent yourself at a criminal trial, jury duty where you cast the deciding vote,
  class-action checks, testifying as a witness, a new bribery charge. 13 events, 7 achievements,
  2 goals, and The Honorable and Serial Litigant legacy titles.

**More story.** 449 events (up from about 200), stories that play out over years, and holidays.
- **Story chains:** 16 stories that pick up again a year or more later, wherever your choices led:
  a stray dog that saves your life, a pen pal you finally meet, a garage band's reunion show, a
  time capsule dug up twenty years on, a neighbor feud that ends in court, a secret half-sibling,
  a friend's startup, an inherited farmhouse (with a ghost if Chaos is on), an old flame, a
  marathon, the office lottery pool, a corporate cover-up, the family recipe, one last road
  trip, a storage-unit auction, and an apprenticeship with an old master.
- **Holidays**, tied to the countries that celebrate them: New Year's, Valentine's Day, Mother's
  and Father's Day, Halloween, Thanksgiving, independence days, Lunar New Year, Chuseok, Diwali,
  Holi, Eid, the Day of the Dead, Carnival, Bonfire Night, St. Patrick's Day, Golden Week, Obon,
  Midsummer, Oktoberfest, Bastille Day, Ferragosto, La Tomatina, Easter, Christmas on the beach
  in the southern hemisphere, and holiday dinners with family (or in prison).
- **Seasons** that follow the climate: first snow and blizzards where it snows, typhoons, monsoons
  and hurricanes where they hit, spring, summer camp, back to school and beach days.
- **Everyday life:** about 190 new events for toddlers, kids, middle schoolers, teens, college
  students (roommates, finals, study abroad, internships), work, love, family and parenting, home
  and money, health, travel, pets, seniors and prison. A few more events happen each year to match.
- **Extras:** 40 holiday and seasonal events, 3 achievements (Storyteller, Festive, Marathoner) and
  a new goal.

**Livelier families.** Your family keeps living around you, and you can see all of it.
- **Extended family:** aunts and uncles (and their spouses), cousins, nieces and nephews. They are
  made the first time you look (the family tree, a family reunion, or "Get to know your extended
  family"), so lives where you never look stay small. Your mother's brothers carry her maiden name,
  and grandparents you never met appear on the tree as having died before you were born.
- **Siblings live their lives:** promotions, weddings you're invited to (give a toast, enjoy the
  party or skip it), divorces, and babies, who become your nieces and nephews. Sometimes they're
  named after you. Cousins and nieces and nephews marry too.
- **In-laws:** meet your partner's family and make a first impression, ask their parents for their
  blessing (it helps the proposal), and see them at the wedding: they stay home if they dislike you
  and chip in if they adore you. Labels follow the relationship: "Partner's mother", "Future
  mother-in-law", "Mother-in-law", "Brother-in-law", "Son-in-law". A breakup or divorce takes them
  with it.
- **Family tree diagram:** both sides of your family, from your grandparents down to your
  grandchildren, with aunts and uncles, cousins, nieces and nephews, and anyone who has died. You can
  open the tree of a friend, partner or spouse too (their relatives are made just for the tree).
- **Across generations:** when you continue as your child, your old siblings become their aunts and
  uncles, and your nieces and nephews become their cousins.
- **Extras:** 10 family events (a sibling on your couch, babysitting, a cousin's wedding, an
  in-law reorganizing your kitchen, the holiday tug-of-war between two families, a family feud...)
  and childless aunts and uncles who may leave you something in their will.

**Parenting.** Raising your kids is full of real decisions now, under Parenting on each child's page.
- **Schools:** when a child turns four, you choose where they'll go: public school, private school
  (about $16,000 a year in the US), boarding school (about $42,000, and they live away from home), or
  homeschool, where results depend on your own smarts. You can switch schools until they're 16.
  Private and boarding schools raise smarts faster and send more kids to university. Boarding kids
  drift further from you. Bright kids can win scholarships. Tuition is due every year, and a child
  moves back to public school if you can't pay.
- **Show business:** push a child (ages 3 to 14) into acting or music. They audition and book gigs,
  from cereal commercials and recitals up to film franchises and platinum singles, and build fame.
  You decide where their pay goes: a trust until they turn 18, half and half, or the family budget.
  Kids can burn out and ask to quit, and making them keep going costs you. The famous ones may go
  pro at 18.
- **Everyday parenting:** help with homework (unless you get it wrong yourself), and five ways to
  discipline them, from a calm talk to yelling.
- **Arranged marriages:** a matchmaker brings three matches for an unmarried adult child: one from a
  wealthy family, one with a great career, and one who is strikingly good-looking. Your child can
  refuse. A match can grow into love or turn miserable.
- **Heirs:** name your heir in Identity & Legal. When you die, they take over your companies and
  your crime family, whichever child you go on to play. Companies you hand to another child carry
  on as rivals on the market. Groom your heir: bring them into the business, or teach them the
  family trade. If you continue as the heir to a crime family, the capos ask whether you'll take
  your parent's seat. Your will can leave everything to your heir too.
- **Continuing as your child:** a former child star starts with fame, acting or music skill and
  their trust fund. A groomed heir starts with business or streetwise skill, and a private-school
  kid stays at their school.
- **Extras:** 17 parenting events (homesick letters from boarding school, keeping up with the
  private-school crowd, homeschool field trips, scholarship offers, talent scouts, tantrums on set,
  the tabloids, young performer awards, jealous siblings, an heir's big idea, a capo's doubts,
  arranged matches that work or don't...) and 5 achievements (Stage Parent, Old School Tie,
  Matchmaker, Succession, Keep It in the Family).

**Relationships 2.0.** Love gets more complicated. Romance actions now have their own section on a
person's page.
- **Open relationships:** ask your partner to open the relationship. Their answer depends on how
  things are between you and on who they are (adventurous partners say yes more often, romantic ones
  less). Partners can ask you, too. While it's open, new partners on the side aren't a secret, but
  jealousy builds with every one of them and can end in an ultimatum or a breakup. Close it again any
  time, and decide whether the others end or carry on in secret.
- **Secret partners:** every affair has a risk of getting caught, shown on the lover's page, that
  grows each year and with every secret rendezvous. Cover your tracks to lower it. When you're
  caught, deny everything, confess and beg, leave your partner for your lover, or suggest opening
  the relationship.
- **One-night stands come back:** the person from a hookup stays in your life. Hook up again, ask
  them on a real date, or ghost them. Years later they turn up as your new manager, your new
  neighbor, your friend's or sibling's date, a viral thread about you, a face at your partner's
  work party, or with a child who has your eyes.
- **Extras:** 19 romance events (an ultimatum from a lover, being spotted across town, a suspicious
  partner, a private investigator, double-booking Valentine's Day...), 3 of them tagged `mature`
  (blackmail, a lover on your doorstep, a fling who won't go away), and 5 achievements (Open Book,
  Smooth Operator, Busted, It's Complicated, Ghosts of Flings Past).

**Taboo family romance (opt-in).** Consensual adult-only family romance, strictly 18+ both
with no teen path, gated by a master `taboo` switch plus seven subtype switches (parents,
step-parents, siblings, children, grandparents/grandchildren, extended family, in-laws —
all default OFF).
- **Eligibility:** family members become dateable only with master + matching subtype on,
  18+ both, single, and the usual orientation/attraction checks. Dual labels ("Brother ·
  Girlfriend"), blood-safe in-law handling, and blocked parental blessing.
- **Realistic genetics:** real pedigree inbreeding stored on each child (`p.inbred`,
  `p.tabooDepth`): unrelated 0, cousin 0.0625, sibling/parent-child 0.25, repeat inbred
  matings climb fast. Low first-generation defect/miscarriage risk, rising sharply later.
- **Neutral sim with fallout:** karma/fame dings, family disapproval and discovery/holiday/
  pregnancy-reaction events. No jail, no teen content, ever.

**Cities.** Where you live matters now. All 160 cities have their own size, cost of living, pay,
big industries and character (capital, coastal, beaches, tourist hotspot, college town,
nightlife, snowy, historic, industrial), shown on a new City page.
- **Money:** rent, home prices and living costs follow the city, so New York costs about twice
  what Phoenix does and pays about 35% more. Each city's housing market booms and slumps, and
  homes gain or lose value with it.
- **Jobs:** big cities post more openings, a city's big industries almost always hire, and small
  cities rarely need developers, scientists or bankers.
- **Neighborhoods:** four per city, from rough to upscale, with names in the local language.
  Rentals and homes for sale come with a neighborhood that sets the price, how safe it is, and how
  you feel living there. Neighborhoods can gentrify, and a new train line raises prices.
- **Moving:** move to another city in your country. Your spouse and kids come along, your partner
  might, your friends miss you, and your job may transfer you (with pay for the new city) or not.
  Job transfers and emigrating use the same moves. Moving into a home you own in another city moves
  you there.
- **Landlords:** every rental has a named landlord with a style (easygoing, hard to reach,
  penny-pinching, a property company, a small-time owner) and online reviews you can check before
  signing. They raise the rent each year, skip repairs, and serve eviction notices: the building
  sold, a relative moving in, a "renovation", vacation rentals, unpaid rent, or payback after you
  called the inspector. Fix things yourself, keep asking, call the housing inspector, withhold rent,
  sue them, fight an eviction in housing court, ask for cash to leave, or win them over with a gift
  basket. Deposits come back, or don't (sue for it). Strong tenant laws in Germany, France and
  Sweden cap raises at 3% and make evictions hard; some other countries are in between.
- **Being a landlord:** rent out any home, or live in one unit of a duplex, fourplex or apartment
  building and rent out the rest. Pick from three named applicants, with a background check if you
  want the truth. Tenants have personalities (model tenants, settled families, late payers, people
  who are hard on the place, loud ones, and professional non-payers), happiness, rent and debts.
  Set the rent below, at or above market, handle repair requests (fix it, patch it or ignore it and
  risk fines, rent strikes and lawsuits), give late payers time or a payment plan, evict, or pay
  people to leave. A property manager takes 8% and handles everything.
- **Extras:** 34 events (commutes, subway delays, star sightings, small-town gossip, floods,
  protests, tourists, startup recruiters, gentrification, landlords who let themselves in, rent
  strikes, tenants who want a dog, sublet to tourists or vanish in the night...), 6 achievements
  (City Hopper, Big City Dreams, Landlord, House Hacker, Tenant Power, Slumlord), 2 goals and the
  Real Estate Baron legacy title.

**Real currencies.** Every country shows money in its own currency: dollars, pounds, euros,
pesos, reais, kronor, yen, won, rupees (in lakhs and crores), naira, rand and more, 14 in all.
Prices never jitter, because each currency keeps a fixed rate for display. A Settings switch
shows everything in US dollars instead.
- **Strength:** each currency gets stronger or weaker every year, drifting back toward normal,
  with the odd crash for the most volatile ones. The Finances page shows today's exchange rate and
  how strong your money is.
- **Emigrating:** your cash is exchanged at today's rate, less a 2% bank fee, and the list of
  countries shows what your cash would become in each. Moving while your money is strong pays;
  inside the eurozone nothing changes.
- **Foreign savings:** hold savings in any other currency (1.5% to buy or sell). They gain when
  that currency strengthens against yours, count toward your net worth, and become cash, free,
  if you move to that country.
- **Travel:** trips abroad cost more when your money is weak and less when it's strong.
- **Extras:** 4 events (a currency slide, a surge, a jar of old foreign coins, a terrible airport
  exchange booth) and 3 achievements (Citizen of the World, Perfect Timing, Forex Fanatic).

## Next up

Roughly in priority order. Each one is a self-contained expansion. Many items include the most
requested features from the BitLife community.

1. **Chaos mode** (`chaos` tag). Random events that grant superpowers (laser eyes, flight...),
    superhero and supervillain careers, ascending as an archangel or a demon lord. Vampire
    lives: feeding, slow aging, hunters, and turning others.
2. **Challenges.** Generated challenge goals and starting scenarios ("born broke, retire rich").
    A daily challenge with streaks. Every challenge stays available, free.
3. **Pet depth.** Pet shows, horse racing, exotic pets, breeding.
4. **More business types.** A casino (house edge, high rollers, cheating scandals) and a zoo
    (collect animals, build exhibits, breeding programs), both on the business engine.
5. **Military depth.** Wars, desertion, veterans' benefits.
6. **Secret agent.** A spy agency career: recruitment, missions abroad, cover identities,
    double agents, and getting burned.
7. **Cults.** Start a movement, attract followers, run a commune, collect donations, and
    dodge defectors, journalists and raids.
8. **Hustles & hobbies.** Outdoor life (hiking, camping, fishing, hunting, national parks) and
    bigger street hustles (a food cart, reselling sneakers, selling knockoffs).
9. **Space missions.** Astronauts fly real missions: launches, spacewalks, space station
    stays and things going wrong in orbit.
10. **Power.** Turn a presidency or a crown into a dictatorship: propaganda, secret police,
    purges, coups and declared wars. Builds on Royalty and Military depth.
11. **Time Machine & themes.** Rewind to any earlier year, not just the last 10, using a
    snapshot every few years so saves stay small. A few extra color themes.
12. **Accessibility pass.** Screen-reader labels throughout, color-blind-safe bars.
13. **Optional AI flavor.** Perchance's `ai-text-plugin` could narrate year summaries or custom events, if wanted.

## Someday

**Historical eras.** Living through the Middle Ages, a revolution or the world wars would mean
replacing nearly every data table (jobs, prices, assets, events, medicine). That makes it a
spin-off for the distant future, not an expansion.

## Not planned

Human trafficking as a crime path.
Messy family drama that isn't sexual (custody battles, feuds, disownment) is fair game.
Consensual adult-only family romance exists as the opt-in `taboo` system above (18+ both,
master + subtype switches, all default OFF) — anything involving minors stays out, always.
